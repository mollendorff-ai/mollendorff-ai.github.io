Five questions where one number is the wrong answer.

A calculator gives one number. These questions have a range, a chance, a rival or a choice in turn behind them. Each page gives the answer, how sure it is, and the model itself, to open and change in your browser.

  • Higher salary or stock options: which job offer pays more?By the recruiter’s story the options are worth 388,750. In 85 futures out of 100 they are worth nothing.
  • Pay down the mortgage or put the money in a TFSA?At a 6% return the TFSA ends ahead by 2,793, but it is ahead in only 59 futures out of 100.
  • How many meals a day does a food truck need to break even?55.2 meals a day if the season goes to plan, but at that figure the truck makes money in only 28 seasons out of 100.
  • A competitor cut prices: should I match?Match when more than a quarter of your customers would leave. If you don’t know how many, matching pays in 66 futures out of 100.
  • Accept the settlement offer or go to court?Take the 40,000: refusing is worth 33,800 on average, and only wins above a 65.4% chance of winning.

These pages are made with Forge, an open-source engine that calculates financial models written as plain text. Each model is a file in Forge’s examples, and its figures are checked against calculations written outside Forge.
