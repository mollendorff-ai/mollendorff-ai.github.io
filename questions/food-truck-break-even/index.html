How many meals a day does a food truck need to break even?

About 55 meals a day, if the season goes to plan. But no season goes to plan: a truck that sells 55 meals a day makes money in only 28 seasons out of 100. To make nine seasons in ten profitable it needs about 68.5 a day, a quarter more.

The truck

A meal sells for 14. After the card fee (2.6%) and the food (4.90), each meal leaves 8.736. A day out costs 250 (a helper, fuel, the pitch), and the season costs 26,000 whatever happens (the truck’s lease, insurance, permits, the kitchen). The season is planned for 130 days, and the plan is 55 meals a day.

Two things are not known. Some days the truck stays in because of rain, a breakdown or a cancelled event: 18 on the plan, but anywhere from 8 to 40. And a day out sells what the street gives: seldom more than the plan, often less (between 70% and 115% of it).

Why break-even is not enough

On the plan the truck is out 112 days and the costs are 54,000, so the break-even is 55.19 meals a day: the usual answer, fixed costs divided by what a meal leaves. But the plan is a middle that is rarely met. Bad days cost the truck more than good days earn it, so the average season at 55 meals a day loses about 3,300, and only 28 seasons in 100 make money.

What a day out sells decides the season far more than the weather does. To make 90 seasons in 100 profitable, the truck must plan for 68.5 meals a day. That is the number a lender or a partner should ask for.

What it assumes

  • The costs, the price and the fee are fixed; only the days lost and what a day sells are uncertain.
  • Every meal sold is the same dish at the same price.
  • A truck that loses money in a season has the cash to carry on to the next.

All figures are made up, for illustration. This is not financial, legal or tax advice.
