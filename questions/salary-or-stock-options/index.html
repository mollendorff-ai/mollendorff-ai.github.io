Higher salary or stock options: which job offer pays more?

Take the salary, unless you can afford to lose it. By the recruiter’s story (the company sells for 300 million) the options are worth 388,750 against the 100,000 of pay you give up. But that is one future out of many, and in most of them the options pay nothing.

The two offers

A startup pays 25,000 a year less than another employer and gives you 25,000 options, which vest over four years. Each option lets you buy a share for $2. The company has 10,000,000 shares. Its investors put in 30,000,000 and get that back first when the company is sold, and later investors will take between 20% and 50% of the company before then, most likely 35%.

How is the company sold? In this model, 60 times in a hundred it fails and brings nothing; 25 times it sells for 50,000,000; 12 times for 300,000,000; 3 times for 1,500,000,000. Those chances are made up, and they are the first thing to change.

How sure is the answer?

Fairly sure about the shape, not about the numbers. Run through 10,000 simulated futures, the options are worth 116,519 on average, only about 16,500 more than the salary you gave up. That average hides two very different results: in 85 futures out of 100 you are 100,000 behind, and in the other 15 you are ahead by 187,500 or more, in the luckiest by about 2,700,000.

Even a sale at 50,000,000, which a quarter of the companies in this model reach, pays nothing: after the investors take their 30,000,000 and later investors take their share, each share is worth less than the $2 you must pay for it.

What matters most is the price the company sells for, by far; how much later investors take hardly matters at all.

What it assumes

  • The options are exercised and the shares sold the day the company is sold, and you stay the four years.
  • No tax, no costs of exercising, and nothing for the other benefits of either job.
  • The chances of each sale are the writer’s guesses, not data about startups.

All figures are made up, for illustration. This is not financial, legal or tax advice.
